# Architecture-significance lens — template

The **seed** for the wrap-up sweep — **step 1** of the finalize gate. This is not any product's lens; it's the example clusters the sweep derives *from*. The product's real lens is written to a separate `<slug>-architecture-lens.md` artifact (see [`finalizing.md`](finalizing.md) → lens artifact format). [`finalizing.md`](finalizing.md) → Architecture-significance sweep covers what the sweep is, the lens-not-a-layer rule, and how it differs from the parking lot; this file is just the corners to walk. For each dimension ask privately: *is there a user whose need on this axis we haven't captured yet?* — if yes, offer one candidate use-case in plain user language and let the user keep, sharpen, or drop it.

**The invariant is the question, not the list.** The one thing that always holds: *which decisions are cheap to make now and expensive to discover after building starts?* Those one-way doors — the ones that quietly fix the architecture, the platform, the data model — are what the sweep hunts, whatever the product. A product's defining door is often one no list below names: a hypervisor's partitioning of cores and devices between guests, a simulator's fidelity-vs-speed trade, a control stack's redundancy failover, a game's authored-vs-procedural content split, a payroll system's handling of a mid-year tax-law change. The dimensions below come in two tiers: a **cross-cutting spine** to walk for *every* product, then **family-specific** clusters — pick the one that fits. The family-specific doors of a consumer app, a real-time control library, and a game are almost disjoint — the spine is the only part they share, so don't force a product through the wrong cluster.

## Cross-cutting dimensions — walk these for every product

These doors recur in almost every product; only the clothes change. Ask them whatever family the product belongs to, then move to the fitting cluster below. Each line: the private question → an example of the **user-POV use-case** it might surface. The examples deliberately span *different* products — the dimension is what's generic, not the domain; phrase yours for the product in front of you.

- **Execution targets / platforms** — which OSes, RTOSes, device classes, browsers, consoles, or stores must it run on — now and later? Adding a target after you built for one is a classic expensive-late move. → *(comms library)* "As a team shipping on both Linux and VxWorks, I can finally run the exact same stack on each without rewriting our integration."
- **Scale & volume** — someone at the top of the range: an enormous pile, power-user throughput, huge node counts, a vast world? → *(photo library)* "As someone with 100,000 photos, I can finally scroll through them all without it ever stuttering."
- **Interop & integration** — must it exchange data with systems already in place (import/export, no lock-in), and is it something others build on through APIs, bindings, or config formats? → *(project tracker)* "As a team leaving another tool, I can finally bring our whole history in — and take it back out if we ever move on."
- **Data location & privacy** — where may data live; what can't leave the device or reach a vendor; any data-residency or regulatory limits? → *(health journal)* "As someone tracking my health, I can finally keep every entry on my own device, never on someone's server."
- **Security & trust boundaries** — who might attack or abuse it, where the trust boundary sits, and what must stay safe even *after* something is breached? The canonical expensive-late door — bolt-on security is a byword for pain, and it's distinct from privacy (this is adversary resistance, not who-may-see). Wears different clothes per family: account and data-breach resistance, stolen API keys and funds on a trading bot, network-reachable actuators on a controller, cheating and account theft in a game, secure boot and signed firmware on a device. → *(online bank)* "As someone who banks online, I can finally trust that even if the company is breached, no one can move my money without my say-so."
- **Reach — languages / regions / abilities** — other languages, regulations, accessibility and assist needs? → *(online store)* "As a shopper who reads Arabic, I can finally browse and check out entirely in my own language, right to left."
- **History & longevity** — needing years of the past, a permanent record, or an audit trail? → *(accounting app)* "As a business owner, I can finally pull up an invoice from seven years ago the moment an auditor asks."
- **Diagnostics & observability** — when something goes wrong, can you capture, trace, and explain what the system actually did — and get that into the right hands? Cheap to design in, brutal to retrofit. Wears different clothes per family: crash reports and support logs, gameplay telemetry, a decision audit, wire/protocol traces. → *(collaboration app)* "As someone whose upload keeps failing, I can finally see exactly what went wrong and hand support a report that pinpoints it, instead of 'try again later.'"
- **Acting on its own (autonomy)** — does the product take actions by itself, not just present options — while the user is away, or faster than a human could act? How much rope does it get, and when must it ask first? Wears different clothes per family: an assistant booking on your behalf, a controller driving a machine unattended, a bot placing trades. → *(personal assistant)* "As someone away for the weekend, I can finally let it handle the routine bookings on its own and just brief me on the rest."
- **Trust, control & override** — the counterweight to any autonomy: can the human inspect, undo, veto, or override what the product did on its own, with limits and a stop it must always honour? Costly to bolt on late. Wears different clothes per family: undo/preview of an automated edit, an approval gate before an assistant acts, a manual override or e-stop on a controller, a kill-switch and position limits on a trading bot. → *(automation controller)* "As the operator, I can finally hit stop and take manual control the instant something looks wrong, and trust the machine obeys at once."

## Family-specific dimensions — consumer / prosumer software

The corners left once the spine is walked — the ones peculiar to interactive, person-in-the-loop software.

- **Offline / flaky connection** — can someone need to act with no network? → *(field-survey app)* "As a surveyor working deep in the mountains, I can finally record everything with no signal, and it catches up the moment I'm back in range."
- **More than one person** — sharing, delegation, handoff, two people on the same thing at once? → *(design tool)* "As a designer handing a project to a teammate, I can finally pass it over with both of us always seeing the latest version."
- **Across devices & channels** — start on one device/channel, continue on another? → *(reading app)* "As someone who starts on my phone and finishes on my tablet, I can finally pick up on the exact page I left."

## Family-specific dimensions — embedded / real-time / developer tools & libraries

For a control stack, communication library, device firmware, or engineering/diagnosis tool, the expensive-late corners left after the spine are almost disjoint from the consumer set. The output is *still* a plain use-case in the voice of whoever depends on the product — an integrating engineer, an operator, a machine builder — never an architecture note.

- **Real-time & determinism** — must it act within a hard time bound, cycle after cycle, with bounded jitter? → *(motion-control stack)* "As a machine builder, I can finally count on every cycle landing on time, even under heavy load, so the axes never lose sync."
- **Hardware, topology & redundancy** — which controllers/buses/wiring, and what happens when a link or node drops? → *(fieldbus master)* "As a plant engineer, I can finally have the line keep running when a cable fails, with a redundant path taking over unnoticed."
- **Safety & certification** — functional-safety requirements, standards, and the evidence trail a certifier demands? → *(safety-rated product)* "As a supplier to a certified machine, I can finally hand the auditor every record they need without reconstructing it by hand."

## Family-specific dimensions — hedonic / experiential / creative products

For a game, a toy, an entertainment or creative product, the expensive-late corners left after the spine are shaped by the *experience*, not by utility — but they are just as much one-way doors, and just as easy to under-decide early. The output is *still* a plain use-case in the voice of whoever lives the experience — a player, a creator, a spectator — phrased as a desire fulfilled ("I get to …"), never an architecture note.

- **Save, progress & continuity** — where does progress live; can someone stop anywhere and resume, across sessions and devices? → *(adventure game)* "As someone who plays in short bursts, I can finally stop wherever I am and pick up exactly there later, on any of my devices."
- **Difficulty & pacing** — can the challenge and intensity bend to different players without breaking the experience? → *(action game)* "As a player who finds the fights too hard, I can finally dial the challenge to where it's fun for me and still get the whole story."
- **Authored vs. emergent content** — handcrafted moments, procedural/systemic generation, or both — and how much the world surprises you on replay? → *(roguelike)* "As someone who's finished it once, I can finally start again and have the world unfold differently every time."
- **Creation & modding** — can players make their own content and share it, and does it play like first-party content? → *(sandbox game)* "As a player who loves building, I can finally craft my own levels and hand them to friends, and they play just like the built-in ones."
- **Shared presence & social** — solo, co-op, or massively shared; are other real people in the world with you? → *(multiplayer game)* "As someone who'd rather not play alone, I can finally drop a friend into my world and we live the moment together."
- **Living service vs. finished artifact** — does it ship once and stay put, or keep evolving with events, seasons, and new content after launch? → *(live game)* "As a long-time player, I can finally come back months later to something fresh, not a world frozen where I left it."
- **Immersion ceiling vs. reach** — the sensory/performance high end (VR, haptics, rich audio) versus running well on modest hardware? → *(game)* "As someone on an older machine, I can finally get the same feel and story, just with the visuals scaled to what I've got."
- **Expression & making it mine** — can someone leave their mark — avatar, space, style — and carry it with them? → *(creative game)* "As a player, I can finally shape a character and a space that feel unmistakably mine and take them everywhere I play."

(A small product may light up two axes, a broad one most — stop at **saturation**.)
