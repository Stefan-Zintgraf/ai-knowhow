### ARTICLE00_SUBJECT
Evaluating LinkLayerRefClock jitter

### ARTICLE00_BODY
<div>
<p>Cyril,</p>
<p> </p>
<p>We have EC-Master running in the LinkLayerRefClock DCM mode. My timestamp granularity is 16ns. I have captured 30 seconds of data on the SysTimeDiff from the first DC slave (an EL4002) in my simple network.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Here is what that data looks like (horizontal times are in milliseconds):</p>
<p><img style="max-width:100%;width:7.7291in;max-height:4.677in;width:742px;max-height:449px;" src="/api/v1/ticket_attachment/3073/16199/64207?view=inline"></p>
<p> </p>
<p>As you can see, most of the times are within +/-100ns or +/-200ns, but we do get occasional spikes in the range of 3000-4000ns. How will this amount of jitter affect the slaves? I know that for BusShift, you say that the RefClock will jitter by 1000ns, but the subsequent DC clocks will jitter by 20ns. Does this mean that a 3000ns jitter might cause a 60ns bump in the actual local clocks? Do you have a recommended way of measuring the effect on the slaves?</p>
<p> </p>
<p>I have attached the full data in an Excel spreadsheet. Is there other information you would like to see to help answer my question?</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Thank you.</p>

<p>I think that it would have no impact regarding the "data set". All the slaves see the same value and will react in the same way. There will be no jitter between the slaves.</p>

<p>The slaves behave with a strong inertia (they need about 16000 frames during the ARMW burst at startup). In your diagram, the jitter seems to happen for 500ms. I don't think that the impact is from interest.</p>
</div>
<div>
<p>For the application, it could make sense to check on the oscilloscope a long term measurement of the SYNC0 and the Start Of Frame signal.</p>
