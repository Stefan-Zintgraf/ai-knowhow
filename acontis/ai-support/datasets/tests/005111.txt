<p>Hi Everyone,</p>
</div>
<div>
<p> </p>
</div>
<div>
<p>Thanks for your time today and glad we got this all resolved for you. Just want to summarize the core issue and what we did to resolve.</p>
</div>
<div>
<p> </p>
</div>
<div>
<p>The acontis software (like many Windows networking software applications) relies on a Windows NDIS Filter Driver (see Wikipedia topic on this) to access the Ethernet controller hardware. Years ago, prior to Windows 10, WinPcap was the most popular NDIS Filter driver for this. Our software relied on WinPcap to operate. Some years ago, the WinPcap development was stopped (see WinPcap site about this notice from 2018).</p>
</div>
<div>
<p> </p>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<p>As Windows 10 came out, they introduced new NDIS specifications that WinPcap never updated to (because development stopped). A fork of WinPcap emerged called Npcap, which enabled backwards compatibility in Windows 10 for applications that used to rely on WinPcap.</p>
</div>
<div>
<p> </p>
</div>
<div>
<p>So the core issue was that WinPcap wasn't working on your recently updated Windows 10 machines, and the acontis software was trying to use WinPcap. The solution was to install Npcap (with the "WinPcap compatibility mode" checkbox checked) and now everything works as expected.</p>
</div>
<div>
<p> </p>
</div>
<p>Due to all this confusion around NDIS filter drivers, WinPcap, and Npcap, acontis now has their own NDIS filter driver that we maintain and support. We recommend using this, but you will need to first update your EC-STA application to a more recent version (yours is several years old). This will also require software development on your end to do these updates, so that may not be possible currently. If you do get some resources and time to do this, please consider changing from WinPcap/Npcap to the acontis NDIS filter driver.</p>
</div>
