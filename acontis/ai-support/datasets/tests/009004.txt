Hi Takuya,<div><br></div><div>
<span>1:
When transitioning to OP mode, what factors could cause an out of sync situation
during a period of normal operation?</span><br>
</div><div>
<span>Answer: </span><span>A deviation of the clocks can have various reasons. The most common reason is probably too narrow limits for the permitted deviation. The second most common is probably a (temporarily) “too slow or too fast master clock”, due to an incorrectly configured operating system (energy management, displacement of the master stack process from the CPU by other processes). The same applies in principle to slaves, but is somewhat less likely here (as there is usually no OS and no parallel processes). Delays when sending or forwarding messages. Programming errors in the master or in the slaves that lead to changed program runtimes in certain situations/cases. Message loss, etc.</span>
</div><div><br></div><div>2: Is
it possible that the PDO data (for example, encoder values from the driver) at
the timing when out of sync is detected contains invalid values?</div><div><div>
<span>Answer: </span><span>Yes, that is in principal possible. However, temporal behavior and sent values must initially be considered independently. If masters and/or slaves are “out-of-sync”, the values are in a different temporal context.</span>
</div></div><div><span><br></span></div><div>
3: Could you explain in simpler terms the settings for deviation limit and
settle time mentioned in the explanation in the photo '1.1.5 Sync Window
Monitoring'?"<br>
</div><div>Answer: <span>The deviation may not be greater than the specification over a certain period of time. </span><span>e.g.: dwInSyncLimit is 4000ns and dwInSyncSettleTime is 1500ms (which are both the default values). </span><span>Then the clock deviation over a duration of 1500ms must not be greater than 4000ns in order to be “in-sync”. After that, any deviation above 4000ns, however small, leads to “out-of-sync”. If you are in “out-of-sync”, the deviation must again be less than 4000ns over the duration of 1500ms in order to get “in-sync” again.</span>
</div><div><span><br></span></div><div>
<p>4: I would also like you to explain in
simpler terms the meaning of the EC_T_INT nCtlSetVal setting.</p>
<p>Answer: <span>nCtlSetVal defines when the SYNC0 signal is to be triggered within a cycle. At the beginning, in the middle or at the end. In our demos, we select SYNC0 at 66% of the cycle time.</span></p>
<p><span>EcMasterDemoDC EcDemoApp.cpp around line 402:</span><br></p>
<div>EC_T_INT nCtlSetValNsec = dwCycleTimeNsec * 2 / 3 /* 66% */; /* distance between cyclic frame send time and DC base on bus */</div>
<div>
<span>So frame is sent, frame is received and then a bit later the SYNC0 is to become signaled. The nCtlSetVal time says when.</span><br>
</div>
