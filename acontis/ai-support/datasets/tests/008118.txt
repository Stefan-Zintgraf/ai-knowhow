<br><div>
<div>The basic requirements are a tight DC with the reference clock on a slave instead of the master.</div>
<div><p> </p></div>
<ol start="1" type="1"><li><span>XFC involves either of two things, <b>time stamping</b> an event independent of the cycle, either through Latch (timestamping) an event at the slave or Sync (interrupting the SSC) interrupting the higher-level code to attend to the event.</span></li></ol>
<div><p> </p></div>
<ol start="2" type="1"><li>
<b>Oversampling</b> (recording multiple values at the slave in between cycle) or generating multiple values at the slave in each cycle. </li></ol>
<div><p> </p></div>
<div>The functionality is built into the XFC Beckhoff terminals; the master just needs to handle it.</div>
<div><p> </p></div>
<div>It requires three things:</div>
<div><p> </p></div>
<ol start="1" type="1">
<li>High cycle determinism</li>
<li>Beckhoff XFC terminals</li>
<li>Historically TwinCAT</li>
</ol>
<div><p> </p></div>
<div>All parameters are in the XFC ESI files but no one else besides Beckhoff has ever implemented it.</div>
<div><p> </p></div>
<div>The Beckhoff XFC terminals have been around for more than 15 years. It also is occasionally used in drives.</div>
<div><p> </p></div>
<div>It is nothing that Acontis cannot handle. Clearly a niche product which is why I was asking.</div>
<div><p> </p></div>
<div>I know the XFC terminals well so let me know if you have any other questions.</div>
