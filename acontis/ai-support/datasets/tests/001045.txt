<p>I am trying to figure out if I am doing something wrong or if this is could be expected behavior. I am trying to understand the ethercat cycle (I have seen it named as such in the documentation) – running the ecatExecJobs.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>The demos and documentation do things like this:</p>
<ol type="1">
<li>Wait for clock tic</li>
<li>Run eUsrJob_ProcessAllRxFrames</li>
<li>Do client stuff/manipulate the process image</li>
<li>Run eUsrJob_SendAllCycFrames</li>
<li>Run eUsrJob_MasterTimer</li>
<li>Run eUsrJob_SendAcycFrames</li>
<li>Goto 1</li>
</ol>
<p> </p>
<p>I have followed this pattern and put all of our processing into step 3. Over all step 3 looks like this: extract new values from process image, update control loops, do whatever else needs doing, and update values in process image.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>For my testing I have inserted busy waits between steps 1 and 2, as step 3, and between steps 6 and 7. I can set the wait times at any time while running. So I can simulate any amount of cpu load at any of these points.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>What I am seeing is that as some point step 3 becomes too long and I start get notification of lost frames. I am getting these notifications at times far less than the complete period. I can easily fill up the total period by adding more work outside of the ethercat cycle (between steps 1&amp;2 or 6&amp;7) without any problem.</p>

### ARTICLE01_BODY
Hi Jon,<div><br></div><div>increasing cpu load at step3 moves <span>eUsrJob_SendAllCycFrames. This job must be called before the critical limit is reached. This limit is the HW delay + communication time it can be determine by using </span><a href="https://developer.acontis.com/ethercat-cycle-time-calculator.html" title="https://developer.acontis.com/ethercat-cycle-time-calculator.html" rel="nofollow noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">https://developer.acontis.com/ethercat-cycle-time-calculator.html</a>.</div><div><br></div><div>Bryan can check if the information match.</div><div><br></div><div>Best Regards,</div><div>Cyril</div><div><br></div>

### ARTICLE02_BODY
<div>Thanks Cyril, I was going to mention this communication time delay to him and point him to the calculator but wanted to make sure I wasn't overlooking something else. In fact, based on his ENI file (1 MII slave, 62 Ebus, 582 bytes in process data), I calculated almost 85us fixed communication time. When you add this to the 15us he calculates for the stack delays, and the additional CPU load time he is adding at step 3, you arrive almost exactly at the network cycle time in each test :).<br>
