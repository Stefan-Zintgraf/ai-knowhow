### ARTICLE00_SUBJECT
Job execution timing

### ARTICLE00_BODY
<div dir="ltr">Hi Jon,<div> </div>
<div>I've a question regarding the expected timing of calling the various eUserJobs. Currently we have it implemented like this:</div>
<div> </div>
<div>We aim for a fixed cycle time with distributed clock synchronization of the devices on the bus. At the start of a cycle, we call eUsrJob_MasterTimer, immediately followed by eUsrJob_ProcessAllRxFrames. Then our application performs some calculation on that data, which may vary a little in time. Sometimes this may be 200us, sometimes 250, give or take. Once the calculation is done, we call eUsrJob_SendAllCycFrames, immediately followed by eUsrJob_SendAcycFrames. We then determine the number of microseconds still left of the cycle time and sleep for that amount of time. The next cycle will then start as described above.</div>
<div> </div>
<div>Is this scheme in any way problematic? In particular, while the time between two eUsrJob_MasterTimer calls can be expected to be constant (=cycle time), the time between two calls of</div>
<div>
<div>eUsrJob_SendAllCycFrames varies depending on how long our application was calculating.<br>
</div>
<div> </div>
<div>Which job is taking care of the distributed clock sync, and is it robust for not always being called in fixed cycles?</div>
<div> </div>
<div>The calculations will always be fast enough, though, that the beginning of a cycle is never delayed.</div>
<div> </div>
<div>Thanks in advance!</div>
<div> </div>

Hi Sebastian,<div><br></div><div><div>eUsrJob_MasterTimer doesn't have to be called very accurate. It execute the internal state machines and process the API calls.
